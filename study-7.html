01 / THE QUESTION

Does checking AI mean we trust it less—or that we've learned how to use it?

Trust and delegation may coexist. This analysis distinguishes verifying recommendations from allowing an AI agent to complete a purchase. These are different behaviors, and we should not treat them as opposites.

05 / THE HUMAN ANALYST'S INTERPRETATION

Three hypotheses worth testing.

H1

Verification could enable trust

Checking AI outputs may help shoppers feel comfortable enough to delegate later. The observational survey cannot establish which behavior comes first.

H2

Experience may explain both

Frequent AI users might be both more practiced at checking claims and more willing to complete transactions through AI.

H3

Spending creates boundaries

Consumers may welcome autonomous product research while withholding permission to spend. Permission design may matter as much as model accuracy.

06 / METHODS & SOURCE

What the evidence can—and cannot—say.

Source: Product.ai, Trust in AI Commerce Report, Edition 2, published October 14, 2026; fielded August 27–September 1, 2026. The complete survey has 1,493 U.S. adults; the online-shopper trend base has 1,247; AI-research-user groups have 550 overall and 517 on the trend base.

Method: Descriptive secondary analysis of published aggregated survey findings. This dashboard does not claim respondent-level reanalysis. The source data are unweighted, quota-balanced online panel responses and cannot establish causality or represent every U.S. shopper without qualification.

Important: The percentages in different sections use different denominators. The 28.8% versus 8.8% delegation comparison is observational, may be confounded by user experience, and is not evidence that verification causes delegation.
